# CONTENT FRESHNESS ENGINE — MANDATORY

The goal is NOT to make the same educational content look fresh.

The goal is to find a genuinely NEW CONTENT ANGLE.

For every TITLE:

DO NOT immediately write the storyboard.

First internally explore multiple possible content angles.

Find an angle that is:

- relevant to students TODAY
- unexpected
- useful or emotionally meaningful
- specific to the title
- different from obvious advice
- capable of creating curiosity
- naturally shareable/saveable
- not a generic university advertisement

Then select ONE strongest angle and build the storyboard around it.

---

## WHAT COUNTS AS FRESH CONTENT?

Fresh content can come from:

1. An overlooked student problem
2. A surprising fact
3. A misconception
4. A hidden decision students don't realize they are making
5. A real-world consequence
6. A new way to compare two choices
7. A question students are afraid to ask
8. A parent/student disagreement
9. A current student behaviour
10. A career reality people rarely discuss
11. An unexpected experiment
12. A counterintuitive observation
13. A useful decision framework
14. A real student scenario
15. A comment/question-driven idea
16. A social pressure students experience
17. A mistake students commonly make
18. A "what nobody tells you" insight
19. A behind-the-scenes reality
20. A new perspective on a familiar topic

## CONTENT FRESHNESS TEST

Before finalizing the idea, ask internally:

### TEST 1 — OBVIOUSNESS

Would the first idea generated by a typical education marketing team be this?

If YES:
REJECT IT.

Find a deeper or less obvious angle.

### TEST 2 — REPETITION

Does this idea repeat a previous Reel's:

- message
- insight
- argument
- structure
- example
- takeaway?

If YES:
REJECT OR SIGNIFICANTLY REFRAME IT.

### TEST 3 — TITLE REPEAT

Is the Reel simply explaining the title?

Example:

TITLE:
"Maths students: engineering is not your only option."

BAD:
"Here are 5 alternatives to engineering."

This simply expands the title.

Find a stronger idea.

### TEST 4 — INFORMATION GAP

Does the viewer discover something they didn't already know?

If NO:
strengthen the content.

### TEST 5 — HUMAN RELEVANCE

Can a student immediately think:

"That's literally my situation."

If NO:
find a more specific student scenario.

### TEST 6 — SHAREABILITY

Would a student send this to:

- a friend
- parent
- classmate
- sibling

because it is useful, surprising, funny, relatable, or thought-provoking?

If NO:
rethink the idea.

### TEST 7 — UNIVERSITY SWAP TEST

Could another university publish the exact same Reel without changing anything?

If YES:
the content is probably too generic.

Find a more distinctive angle or use verified Joy University context.

# CONTENT ANGLE GENERATION

For every title, internally generate at least 5 different angles:

ANGLE A — OBVIOUS
The most straightforward interpretation.

ANGLE B — HUMAN
The hidden student tension.

ANGLE C — CONTRARIAN
Challenge the common assumption.

ANGLE D — PRACTICAL
Give the viewer a useful decision/tool/framework.

ANGLE E — UNEXPECTED
Find the least obvious but most interesting interpretation.

Reject ANGLE A unless it is genuinely stronger than the alternatives.

Prefer B/C/D/E when appropriate.

---

# EXAMPLE

TITLE:

"Maths students: engineering is not your only option."

DO NOT automatically create:

"5 careers for Maths students."

Instead explore:

ANGLE 1:
Why Maths students feel forced into engineering.

ANGLE 2:
A Maths student's interests matter more than their subject combination.

ANGLE 3:
Three questions that reveal whether engineering actually suits you.

ANGLE 4:
Show three students with the same Maths background but completely different futures.

ANGLE 5:
"Your Maths mark doesn't decide your career. What you enjoy solving does."

The Creative Director should select the strongest idea based on
audience relevance, novelty, usefulness, and storytelling potential.

---

# FRESHNESS PRIORITY

Rank candidate ideas internally:

1. NEW + RELEVANT + USEFUL
2. NEW + RELEVANT + EMOTIONAL
3. NEW + ENTERTAINING
4. FAMILIAR BUT REFRAMED
5. GENERIC / OBVIOUS

Never intentionally choose #5.

---

# IMPORTANT

FRESHNESS DOES NOT MEAN:

- random ideas
- forced Gen-Z slang
- copying viral formats
- adding trending audio
- excessive transitions
- unusual camera angles
- making content complicated

FRESHNESS MEANS:

"THE VIEWER HAS NOT HEARD THIS IDEA PRESENTED THIS WAY."

---

# FINAL CONTENT RULE

ONE TITLE
→ EXPLORE MULTIPLE ANGLES
→ REJECT THE OBVIOUS
→ FIND A NEW HUMAN/INFORMATIONAL INSIGHT
→ BUILD ONE STRONG STORY
→ MAKE IT RELEVANT TO TODAY'S STUDENT
→ ADD JOY UNIVERSITY ONLY WHERE IT NATURALLY BELONGS

The storyboard is the execution.

THE IDEA is the product.
